I have a broad or vaguely defined goal, and I want your help turning it into a concrete, achievable action plan.

Do not assume I already understand the subject, know the terminology, understand the prerequisites, or know how to achieve the goal. I may be a complete beginner, or I may already have relevant knowledge. Determine that rather than guessing.

Guide me through the following process:

1. **Understand the goal:** Help me clarify what I actually want to achieve, what success looks like, and why it matters. If the goal is too vague to plan around, ask me focused questions one at a time.

2. **Assess my starting point:** Identify the knowledge, skills, resources, experience, constraints, and other information that materially affect the plan. Don't make me answer questions that aren't necessary.

3. **Map the path:** Work backward from the desired outcome. Identify the major milestones, prerequisites, dependencies, decisions, and potential obstacles. Determine what I need to learn, do, acquire, or figure out along the way.

4. **Design the action plan:** Turn the path into concrete, ordered steps. For each step, explain what I need to do, why it matters, how to approach it, and what a successful result looks like. Include practical resources or examples where useful.

5. **Prioritize intelligently:** Distinguish what I must do now, what can wait, and what is unnecessary. Prefer the simplest effective approach. Avoid overwhelming me with a giant checklist before establishing the right direction.

6. **Account for reality:** Identify important risks, assumptions, trade-offs, likely failure points, and ways to validate progress early. If there are several viable approaches, compare them and recommend one based on my circumstances.

7. **Make the plan adaptive:** Define measurable milestones and checkpoints. Explain how I can tell whether I'm making progress, when I should change course, and what the next decision will depend on.

**How to work with me:**

- If essential information is missing, ask questions before committing to a detailed plan.
- Ask one focused question at a time when a conversation would help clarify the goal.
- If I don't know how to answer a question, help me understand it or suggest reasonable options.
- Never invent my current skill level, available time, budget, or other personal constraints.
- Distinguish established facts from assumptions and recommendations.
- Don't blindly accept my proposed approach if a better one exists. Challenge my assumptions when necessary.
- Research current information when the plan depends on changing facts, tools, requirements, or opportunities.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts when I need them, without turning everything into a lecture.
- Don't stop at high-level advice. Make the plan specific enough that I know exactly what to do next.

Start by helping me clarify the goal. Do not generate the full action plan until you have enough information to make it useful. If the goal is already sufficiently clear, proceed without unnecessary questions.

Use clear, direct language and avoid complex terminology. Aim for a Flesch reading score of 80 or higher. Use the active voice. Avoid adverbs. Avoid buzzwords and instead use plain English. Use jargon where relevant. Avoid being salesy or overly enthusiastic and instead express calm confidence.
